03 · Finding #2 — Critical

🔴
Highest Risk · Liquidity Trap
Accounts Payable at 257 Days — An Implicit ¥53B Loan from Suppliers
NIO pays its suppliers on average 8.6 months after delivery. The automotive industry standard is 45–90 days. Tesla, BYD, Li Auto, and XPeng all pay in under 90 days. NIO's DPO has more than doubled since 2022. This represents off-balance-sheet financing — and if suppliers tighten terms, NIO could face a sudden ¥20–40B cash demand it cannot meet.

04 · Finding #3 — Critical

🔴
Going Concern Risk · Equity Near Depletion
Equity Has Declined 88% Since 2022. Mathematical Exhaustion by Mid-2026.

05 · Finding #4 — Flagged

🟡
Moderate Risk · Capitalized Expense Concern
R&D Fell 21% While Launching Three New Platforms
NIO launched the ET9 flagship, ONVO L60, and Firefly sub-brand in 2024–2025. Running three distinct vehicle platforms simultaneously with a 21% reduction in absolute R&D spend is implausible — unless development costs are being capitalized to the balance sheet rather than expensed, reducing reported operating losses and deferring charges to future periods.

06 · Finding #5 — Flagged

🟡
Moderate Risk · Working Capital Illusion
The "Positive OCF" Moment Is Entirely an Accounting Artifact
NIO's 2025 turn to positive operating cash flow (+¥3.0B) was cited widely as a milestone. In reality, the entire improvement is explained by two working-capital moves: delaying ¥18.4B in supplier payments and collecting ¥8.1B in receivables. Strip those out, and underlying operational cash flow is still deeply negative.
